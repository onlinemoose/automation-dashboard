# Content brief

**Working title direction:** something about meetings going quiet, or the
team that stopped pushing back. Not "how to be a better listener".

**Audience:** engineering managers in their first two years, who were
strong individual contributors and are used to being right.

**Length:** ~1,100 words.

**Angle:** the problem is not rudeness or poor communication skills. It is
a specific, comfortable mistake — arriving at a decision already certain,
and treating pushback as a delay to route around. The piece should make
the reader recognise themselves in it, then give them something concrete
to do.

## Structure to follow

1. **Open on the symptom, not the theory.** The meeting that used to have
   argument in it and now just has nods. The proposal that gets "sounds
   good" instead of questions. Name the false comfort: quiet reads as
   agreement, agreement reads as trust.
2. **Separate this from micromanagement.** Micromanagement is about
   controlling *how* work gets done. This is quieter: the team going
   silent on judgement calls because disagreeing with you stopped being
   worth the effort. Use micromanagement only as the familiar thing to
   contrast against.
3. **Why new managers reach for certainty.** Promoted for being right;
   the reflex that made them a strong engineer (find the answer, defend
   it) misfires in the manager's seat. Keep this short.
4. **The turn: disagreement is information about risk.** An objection is
   data about something you can't see from your new seat — the edge case,
   the operational reality, the history. Suppressing dissent doesn't
   remove the risk, it removes your visibility into it. This is the
   spine of the piece.
5. **What to do instead.** Concrete habits, as a bullet list near the
   end: ask for the strongest objection before deciding; say explicitly
   whether you're gathering input or announcing a decision; treat fast
   unanimous agreement on a hard call as a signal to dig, not relax;
   distinguish real consensus (someone is actually advocating for it)
   from mere absence of dissent.
6. **Close** on the reframe: certainty reached without input isn't
   strength, it's a blind spot. The first-year job isn't to be right
   fastest; it's to build a team where what you can't see gets said.

## Notes

- If research turns up figures on new-manager failure rates or
  psychological-safety studies, use them, but flag anything that traces
  to no named source rather than stating it as fact.
- Avoid turning this into a lecture on empathy. It's about risk and
  judgement, not being nicer.
